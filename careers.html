Join the WICG team

Help us write the next chapter of crypto media.

WICG is in early growth mode. We are not hiring full-time staff yet, but we are actively looking for freelance contributors, expert collaborators, and women who want to write about the topics they know best. If that's you, read on.

Crypto media has a women problem. Of the largest crypto publications, the vast majority of editorial staff are men, the bylines are predominantly male, and the tone of coverage reflects that. We are building the alternative. Our editorial team will eventually be majority women, our bylines will reflect that, and our coverage will be shaped by writers who understand the audience we serve because they are part of it.

If you write thoughtfully about crypto, finance, RWA, DeFi, security, regulation, or related topics, and you want a publication that values careful work over hot takes, we want to talk to you. The roles below are how we work with contributors today. We will add more as the publication scales.

How we work with contributors

If we accept your pitch or invite you to write a commissioned piece, here is what to expect:

  • Clear scope upfront. Word count, angle, deadline, and rate are agreed before you start writing.
  • Standard rates. $200 for 800 to 1,200 word articles. $400 for 1,500 to 2,500 word features. $500+ for in-depth research pieces with primary interviews.
  • One round of edits. We will return your draft with notes within 5 business days. You revise once.
  • Byline always. Every contributor gets a byline and a short bio at the bottom of the piece. We never publish "ghostwritten" content.
  • Payment within 30 days. Invoices processed within 30 days of publication. No 60 or 90 day terms.
  • You retain copyright after a 90-day exclusivity window. Repurpose your piece anywhere you want after that, with attribution.

What we are not looking for

  • AI-generated articles. We can spot them. Submitting them wastes your time and ours.
  • Promotional content about your own project. If you have a product or service, you can still write for us about adjacent topics, but you cannot use editorial as marketing. Sponsorship is the right path for that.
  • Generic crypto news roundups. Anything we could find on five other sites is not interesting to us.
  • Price prediction pieces. If your pitch is "why X will hit $Y by date Z," it's not a fit.
